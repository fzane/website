Quiet Bench

A calm, warm system for a research notebook — serif voice, muted working colors, and the occasional pencil note in the margin.

This page is both the documentation and the reference implementation. The CSS in the PART 1 — THE DESIGN SYSTEM block of this file is normative — including its comments; the budgets written there are rules, not advice. PART 2 styles only this guide. The site this serves mixes visual essays (scroll-scene decks), experiments, and standard pages; everything below applies to all of them unless marked otherwise. Matching the tokens is necessary but not sufficient — the composition rules and color budgets carry equal weight.

1

Principles

  • Informal, not casual. The voice of an enthusiastic tinkerer: lowercase tags, plain words, visible delight — but precise spacing and restrained color. Never LinkedIn-hype, never MBA-deck.
  • Visuals carry the argument. This site is a conscious alternative to the long post. Prefer a figure, a comparison grid, or one strong sentence over a paragraph.
  • Color is labor, not decoration. Four muted working colors, each with one job (§3). A colored word is the emphasis system; backgrounds stay quiet.
  • One thousand no's. Empty space is a feature. Never pad with stats, icons, or filler sections.
  • Composition is the spec too. Correct tokens assembled like a marketing page are still wrong. Every screen should match one of the archetypes in §5–§7; when in doubt, copy an archetype wholesale rather than improvising with the tokens.
2

Typography

Three families, no more. Lora (weight 500, slight negative tracking) is the heading voice. Atkinson Hyperlegible is every other word — body, UI, captions, tags. Caveat appears only as hand notes (§4.3). Monospace exists solely for literal markup (§5.5). No condensed or display faces.

Headings open cold. Nothing ever sits above a heading: no kicker, no eyebrow, no label — uppercase or lowercase, bold or regular, colored or muted. A lowercase muted label above a title is still a kicker. Wayfinding belongs in the running footer (§7.1).

3.1 — The budget (hard rules, per screen or scene)

  • Clay: one moment. One word/phrase, or one accent rule, or one prompt-delta, or one hand note in clay — one of these, total. The second clay element on a screen is a bug.
  • Blue: at most two elements (a tag and a link, say). Blue is never a label, kicker, or heading.
  • Repetition kills color. In any grid or row of repeated elements, the repeated part is ink/neutral. Four prices in four cards are ink — coloring all four clay marks nothing and spends the whole budget. Color marks the exception, not the pattern.
  • Moss and gold appear only in their §5.3 meta-row / topic-chip roles. They never migrate to scenes as decoration.
  • If a screen feels colorful, remove something. Zero working colors on a screen is always acceptable.

4

Emphasis & accents

4.1 — The accent rule

Francis Zane
About

A short, straight 2px stroke under display text — the quiet descendant of a hand-drawn underline. Clay under the site name; moss under interior page titles. Width is 30–60% of the text above it, offset 16–18px below, indented 2–4px. Once per page; in an essay, under the cover title only — never under interior scene titles, never under H2/H3, never full-width. The rule is always horizontal: a colored left border on a quote or card is not an accent rule, it's a banned trope (§9).

4.2 — Colored words

Working notes on AI — experiments first, words second, made to be read.

Six AIs, one two-sentence request. Where the details in AI output actually come from.

Four of six built headphones.

The emphasis system. In sans body text: color + weight 700. In serif (Lora) text: color only, weight unchanged. Blue marks structure ("what this is"); clay marks findings ("what surprised us"). Never use background highlights, marker effects, or underline-as-emphasis. Budget per §3.1: one clay moment per screen.

4.3 — Hand notes (optional, rationed)

results
figure
fig. 1 — six storefronts, one prompt.
started as a bet with myself ↖
nobody asked for headphones!

The one element imported from the "Penciled" exploration. Caveat 21px, rotated −2°, in pencil (#8a8068) or clay for excitement — never gold, never recolored for dark ground (hand notes don't appear on dark scenes at all). Ration: at most one per screen; in an essay, at most one per act and 2–3 per essay total, never on consecutive scenes. Only in margins or beside figures, never inline with body text; never carries essential information (it must be deletable without loss); plain unicode arrows (↖ ← →) may point at what it annotates. A clay hand note counts as the screen's clay moment (§3.1).

4.4 — Numbered sidenotes

Most of what's here started as a question that fit on an index card.1 Instead of answering with a long post, I answer with the kind of thing I'd want to sit through.

1.  Mostly weekend-sized. A weekend is the right size for an honest question.

Tufte-style: a clay superscript in the text, the note in the margin column at 14.5px muted. Use for asides and method details on pages that have a margin column (§6).

4.5 — Quoting long material (briefs, excerpts)

the prompt + a design brief

make a single file html page with a realistic mockup of a product page… use the following design brief:

Design a product page that feels less like a catalog entry and more like a quiet unveiling: spacious, luminous, and meticulously composed. Use a restrained palette, generous negative space, and large-scale imagery…

Quoted long-form material (a brief, an excerpt) is Lora italic 19px in muted, indented behind a 1px hairline left border — never a 2px clay border, never a colored lead-in line. The quote itself carries zero working colors. When showing a prompt that grew (base + addition), the base is muted and the addition is clay — that delta is the screen's entire clay budget; nothing inside the quoted material below it gets colored.

Convergent outputs (invented names, colorways, taglines, prices) are evidence chips: Lora 500 value + lowercase faint attribution on a white card; exceptions go dashed + dimmed, same grammar as §5.4. Monospace is legal only for text that literally appears in source code — a <title> tag qualifies; a price, a chip name, or a tagline does not. Those are quoted artifacts of language, and they're set in serif. Chip values are ink — never clay (§3.1).

6

Page layout

Site pages (landing, about, project indexes) share one skeleton:

  • Content column: max-width 1000px, centered; side padding clamp(28px, 8vw, 100px).
  • Header: wordmark left, nav right, on one baseline. 52px top padding.
  • Hero block: 96px below the header. Display name (or H1 + rule), then the tagline 30px below the rule. The landing tagline is sans body at 20px with one blue emphasis phrase ("…experiments first, words second, made to be read.") — the Lora-italic lead (§2) is reserved for the About page.
  • Section gap: 88px between major blocks. Card grids: 26–30px gaps.
  • Optional margin column: pages that use sidenotes or hand-notes switch the main area to grid-template-columns: 1fr 300px; gap: 64–80px. The aside holds portraits, figures, footnotes, and hand notes. On screens under 900px the aside folds beneath the main column and hand notes are dropped (they're optional by definition).
  • Footer of the about page: a 1px line rule, then external links: GitHub  Email — blue, bold, "↗" suffix for external.

Landing page order: header → hero (name, clay rule, tagline) → "Experiments" H2 with a muted count ("one so far") → experiment cards → "All experiments →". About page order: header → H1 + moss rule → Lora-italic lead → body (with sidenotes in the margin column, portrait card tagged "me, probably") → "currently poking at:" row of category-underlined topics → links rule.

Every other page reuses the same skeleton — same header, same column, H1 + moss rule. Projects index: H1 "Projects", then the same entry cards as the landing list. CV: plain prose under H2 section headings, external links as blue "↗" links — no cards, no working colors beyond that. 404: one body line and a single blue link home; this page is a fine home for one hand note. No page invents new chrome.

7

Scenes — slides & scroll essays

Decks and full-viewport scroll scenes are designed at 1280×720 (16:9); in scroll essays each scene is min-height: 100svh with the same composition rules. Padding: 70px top, 120px sides, 56px bottom. A scene is a composed slide, not an article section that happens to fill the viewport — every scene should match one of the archetypes below.

7.1 — Scene anatomy

  • No kickers, no scene labels. A scene opens with its Lora title, full stop. The narrative beat ("the reveal", "the product") goes into the title or stays in your head. Bold blue lowercase labels are kickers and are banned (§2, §9).
  • Content sits in the middle band, vertically centered or optically so. Evidence (cards, grids) centers horizontally; a lone artifact card centers on the scene's axis, it doesn't hug the left column edge.
  • Running footer: deck title left, "n / N" right — Atkinson 13px faint, both sides. Never serif, never bold. This is the only wayfinding a scene gets.
  • Title scale: Lora 500, 48px at 1280×720 (cover may use display 80). Cap scene titles at ~56px when fluid.
  • One idea per scene. Title + evidence + at most one short summary line (18–19px). If the summary wants to be a paragraph, the scene wants a figure instead.

7.2 — The dark ration

Default scenes are light (paper). The warm dark scene exists for at most two beats per essay — the moments the argument turns — and never on consecutive scenes. An essay that alternates light/dark has spent the device; so has one where a third of the scenes are dark. The prompt/artifact card lives on light paper by default (below); putting it on dark counts against the ration. No hand notes on dark ground, ever.

A text-only beat: one Lora sentence (≤2 lines, ≤1 clay word) and at most one short plain supporting line — no bold pile-ups, no second paragraph. Generous void is the composition. Shown dark because a statement is usually the beat that earns the dark ration (§7.2); the same archetype on paper is the default. 7.6 — Motion & affordances

  • Scenes reveal with a single fade-up (opacity 0→1, translateY 22px→0, 0.7s ease, staggered ~0.15s per element). Total stagger on a scene stays under ~1s. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Scroll cue: "scroll ↓", 13.5px faint, bottom center. Progress dots (if any): 7px, line-dash, active = ink — navigation affordances never use working colors.
  • Dark-ground substitutions: card #322d23, border #46402f, tag flips to light blue #9aa8e0 with dark text, serif emphasis uses #d99a76, card shadow deepens to 0 12px 36px rgba(0,0,0,.35). These are the only dark substitutions — nothing else gets recolored.
8

Voice & copy

  • Pieces are "experiments", never "posts" or "articles". Counts are written out, lowercase: "one so far".
  • Tags are lowercase and conversational: "experiment 01", "me, probably", "the prompt, verbatim".
  • The nav destination is "Projects", but the pieces themselves are experiments: the landing H2 reads "Experiments", entry tags read "experiment 01", and the action link reads "All experiments →" — never "project 01" or "All projects".
  • Dates are written out — "June 2026" — never "2026-06" or "06/2026".
  • Findings are stated as short declaratives with one emphasized word: "Four of six built headphones."
  • Narrative beats ("the reveal", "now look closer") live in titles or in the writing — never as labels above titles.
  • Methods get one honest line, faint, at the end — never a methodology section up front.
  • Hand notes are the author talking to themself, not to the reader: "started as a bet with myself", "nobody asked for headphones!"
9

Don't

  • No kickers or eyebrows of any kind — uppercase or lowercase, bold or regular, colored or muted. Headings open cold.
  • No colored left-borders on quotes, cards, or callouts. The accent rule is horizontal, under display text, once per page (§4.1).
  • No repeated working colors across grids or rows — four clay prices is zero emphasis and four bugs (§3.1).
  • No color changes on hover — hover lifts a card's shadow or underlines a link, nothing else (§5.3).
  • No monospace for non-code. Prices, product names, taglines are serif quotes, not code (§5.5).
  • No more than two dark scenes per essay, never consecutive; the artifact card defaults to light (§7.2).
  • No serif, no bold in the running footer — Atkinson 13px faint only (§7.1).
  • No marker/background highlights — emphasis is colored words only (§4.2).
  • No condensed, tall, or high-contrast display faces; no Inter/Roboto/system-grotesque substitutions.
  • No small-caps labels — tags are lowercase.
  • No pure black, pure white, or cool grays — everything warms toward brown.
  • No saturated versions of the working colors; if a color pops, it's wrong.
  • No emoji, no icon fonts, no decorative SVG illustrations. Unicode arrows (→ ↗ ↖) are the entire icon set.
  • No more than one hand note per screen, one per act, 2–3 per essay; none at all is always acceptable. Never on dark ground, never gold (§4.3).
  • No formal gallery/museum styling: no centered tracked-out captions, no full-black scenes.
